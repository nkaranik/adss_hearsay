{case_id}

- If the decision problem above is concrete and non-empty, use it exactly as the field "phi".

{narrative}

GENERAL OBJECTIVE
Extract all distinct argumentative components that are genuinely relevant to deciding φ.
The goal is not to summarize the case as one argument. The goal is to decompose the reasoning into multiple legally or decision-relevant components whenever the case supports such decomposition.

For LegalBench hearsay / FRE 801(c) cases, decompose the reasoning into components such as:
1. whether the statement was made out of court;
2. whether the statement contains an assertion;
3. whether the statement is offered to prove the truth of the matter asserted;
4. whether the statement is offered for another purpose, such as notice, effect on listener, impeachment, verbal act, or state of mind;
5. whether an exclusion such as party-opponent admission under FRE 801(d)(2) appears relevant;
6. whether an exception-like consideration is present, while keeping the distinction between definitional hearsay and admissibility exceptions clear;
7. whether the evidence is merely circumstantial/non-assertive rather than an out-of-court statement;
8. whether the narrative contains facts that are irrelevant to the hearsay target claim.

For LegalBench contract NLI / confidentiality-of-agreement cases, decompose the reasoning into components such as:
1. whether the contract context contains an explicit confidentiality clause;
2. whether the clause applies to the relevant party or information type;
3. whether the target confidentiality statement is directly entailed by the contract text;
4. whether the target statement is contradicted by the contract text;
5. whether the target statement is merely not mentioned or not inferable from the contract;
6. whether exceptions, carve-outs, duration limits, scope limitations, or permitted disclosures affect the confidentiality obligation;
7. whether definitions such as Confidential Information, Recipient, Disclosing Party, or Agreement scope support or weaken the target statement.

For generic non-legal decision problems, decompose the reasoning into components such as:
1. facts supporting the target claim;
2. facts attacking the target claim;
3. causal links;
4. rule/principle applicability;
5. exceptions, defenses, counterarguments, or alternative explanations;
6. evidence of harm, benefit, liability, feasibility, risk, preference, or uncertainty.

DO NOT INVENT FACTS

- If the case truly contains only one relevant component, return only one argument.

ARGUMENT REQUIREMENTS

ARGUMENT GRANULARITY RULES

STANCE DEFINITIONS
- "support": the argument increases the acceptability of φ.
- "attack": the argument decreases the acceptability of φ.
- "neutral": the component is background or irrelevant to φ.

For hearsay cases:
- Out-of-court + assertion + offered for truth usually SUPPORTS φ.
- Offered for notice/effect on listener/verbal act/impeachment usually ATTACKS φ.
- Party-opponent exclusion under FRE 801(d)(2) usually ATTACKS φ because the statement is treated as non-hearsay.
- Pure circumstantial evidence that does not depend on an out-of-court assertion usually ATTACKS φ.
- Irrelevant facts should be marked NEUTRAL.

For contract NLI cases:
- A clause or definition that directly entails the target confidentiality statement SUPPORTS φ.
- A clause that contradicts, narrows, excludes, or limits the target statement ATTACKS φ.
- Absence of relevant contractual support should usually ATTACK φ if the task is entailment.
- Unrelated contractual provisions should be marked NEUTRAL.

RELATION REQUIREMENTS
Create both:
1. argument-to-claim relations, where arguments support or attack phi;
2. argument-to-argument relations, where one argument supports, attacks, rebuts, undercuts, qualifies, or weakens another argument.

- Every non-neutral argument should normally have a direct relation to "phi", except when its primary role is clearly to support, attack, rebut, undercut, qualify, or weaken another argument.
- In that case, an argument-to-argument relation is sufficient.

You MUST add argument-to-argument relations when:
- a counterargument says that another argument is insufficient;
- a counterargument gives an alternative explanation;
- a rebuttal says that a risk or objection is acceptable, outweighed, or not decisive;
- one argument narrows, qualifies, or limits another argument.

Use relation type:
- "support" when the source strengthens the target;
- "attack" when the source weakens, rebuts, undercuts, or challenges the target;
- "neutral" only when there is no argumentative interaction.

You must infer not only relations between arguments and phi, but also relations between arguments.

An argument attacks another argument when it:
- challenges the sufficiency of that argument;
- challenges the relevance of that argument;
- challenges the causal link from that argument to phi;
- provides an alternative explanation for that argument's evidence;
- weakens the evidential value of that argument;
- rebuts a risk, objection, or counterargument;
- says that a consideration does not automatically imply the conclusion.

When an argument weakens the inference from another argument to phi, encode this as an attack relation from the weakening argument to the original argument.

Examples:
- "Severe symptoms alone do not prove surgery is necessary" attacks an argument saying "Symptoms are severe and persistent" when the latter supports surgery.
- "Falls may have other causes besides spinal stenosis" attacks an argument saying "Function is clearly impaired" or "falls support surgery".
- "Neurologic decline needs objective confirmation" attacks an argument claiming progressive neurologic decline if objective confirmation is not established.
- "Surgical risk may be acceptable given severe disability and failed conservative treatment" attacks an argument saying surgical risks count against surgery.
- "Controlled diabetes and age do not automatically rule out surgery" attacks an argument saying age and diabetes count against surgery.
- "A difficult recovery may still be justified if expected benefit is substantial" attacks an argument saying difficult recovery counts against surgery.

Do not collapse these into generic direct attacks on phi if the target argument can be identified.

STRENGTH / TAU REQUIREMENTS
For each non-neutral argument, assign tau in [0.1, 1.0].
Tau reflects intrinsic strength before QBAF propagation.
Use this rubric:
- relevance: direct relevance to φ;
- factual_grounding: support from the narrative text;
- specificity: case-specific detail;
- logical_coherence: clear inference from fact to claim;
- applicability: fit with the relevant legal/domain principle.
